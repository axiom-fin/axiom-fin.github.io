The open question

A world model assumes that observations arise from a low-dimensional latent state with simple dynamics. The assumption either matches the structure of a domain, and the model trains with less compute, or it does not, and the model loses accuracy to an unconstrained sequence model. We have no way to tell which case applies before training.

TL;DROn one benchmark of 319 equities, a compact latent world model matched or exceeded a Transformer's directional accuracy on raw price data at about 40% of its wall-clock training time. No existing theory explains why. We ask the community to help identify the general principle, if one exists.

Paper

Abstract

We pose an open question about latent world models as an efficiency mechanism, not a solved one. On a Yahoo Finance benchmark of 319 equities (2018–2026), a compact latent world model matched or exceeded a Transformer's directional accuracy on raw price data while training in a fraction of the time. No existing theory explains why imposing explicit latent-dynamics structure should be compute-efficient here, or predicts when it should be preferred over unconstrained attention elsewhere. This single-dataset observation serves only as a concrete anchor; we ask the community to help identify the general principle, if one exists.

Caveat

  • We do not know whether the observations reflect real structure in price dynamics or an artifact of this dataset and this training budget.

Why it is unresolved

  • Scaling laws describe loss against size, data, compute and numerical precision for a fixed architecture. They do not compare inductive biases.
  • Recent Transformer-based world models set performance records for control. That work does not explain why latent-dynamics structure should be efficient for prediction on noisy financial series.

What property of a time series predicts the winner before either model is trained?

Candidates include autocorrelation, regime persistence and effective latent dimensionality.

Q3SizingOpen

Is there a principled way to size the latent dimensionality of a world model?

Such a rule would be analogous to compute-optimal frontiers for model size. With the matching conditions known in advance, a practitioner could choose a small structured model with confidence and skip the empirical comparison.

Ideas, counterexamples and datasets are welcome on the OpenReview forum or by email.
