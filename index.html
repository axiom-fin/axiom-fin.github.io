Towards Financial World Modeling: An Open Efficiency Question

A compact latent world model matched or exceeded a Transformer on raw price data and trained in a fraction of the time. No existing theory explains why. We ask the community to help identify the general principle, if one exists.

The open question

Before either model is trained, what property of a time series predicts that latent-dynamics structure will beat unconstrained attention?

The observation

One benchmark, reported as an anchor and not as a general result

A world model assumes that observations arise from a low-dimensional latent state with simple dynamics. The assumption either matches the structure of a domain, and the model trains with less compute, or it does not, and the model loses accuracy to an unconstrained sequence model. We have no way to tell which case applies before training.

We do not know whether the observation reflects real structure in price dynamics or an artifact of this dataset and this training budget.

Why it is unresolved

Current theory compares sizes, not inductive biases

Scaling laws describe loss against model size, data, compute and numerical precision for a fixed architecture. They do not compare inductive biases, so they cannot say which bias is compute-optimal for a given data-generating process.

Recent Transformer-based world models set performance records for control. That work does not explain why latent-dynamics structure should be efficient for prediction on noisy financial series. If the matching conditions were known in advance, a practitioner could choose a small structured model with confidence and skip the empirical comparison.

Three sub-questions for the community

Which property predicts the winner?

Candidates include autocorrelation, regime persistence and effective latent dimensionality, each measured before either model is trained.

Does the observation extend?

The open cases are assets beyond large-cap equities and time series outside finance.

How large should the latent state be?

A principled rule for latent dimensionality would play the role that compute-optimal frontiers play for model size.
